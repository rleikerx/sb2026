# 11 Aug 2026 — the joint frame, and everything downstream of it

`ArcSkeleton.pose()` never read `bone.axis`. One missing conjugation was the whole of the
universal backward lean *and* the Aracoix wing fold, and closing it moved five other things
that had been quietly calibrated against the broken pose.

## What was wrong

`pose()` composed `parent * clip_quat`. `docs/CLIENT_BINARY_FINDINGS.md` had the ASF axis in
its **ruled-out** table — 40 composition variants scored, "best 12.84 vs 16.11 current" —
so the family had been searched and dismissed.

It was dismissed on the wrong number. The search ranked on `pose_invariants.py`'s aggregate,
which averages in `arm` — a weak invariant — over rigs that are allowed to stand hunched.
The aggregate moves 15.84 → 12.17 for the correct fix, which reads as a near-miss. The one
number anchored to client video moves **17 degrees**.

## The client settles it

`sb.exe 0x5d78c0`, recursing through `0x5d7ba0`, walks the skeleton once at load and per bone
reads the raw axis at `bone+0x30`, runs `Quaternion::MakeTripleRotate` on it, and caches:

| offset | contents |
|---|---|
| `0x90` | `C = MakeTripleRotate(axis)` |
| `0xa0` | `C^-1` |
| `0xb0` | the **parent's** `C^-1` |

Caching `C_parent^-1` *on the child* is ASF's `rot_parent_current`. So the chain is
`A_i = A_parent * C_parent^-1 * C_i * R_i`, which in world-space terms is the conjugation
`G_i = G_parent * (C_i R_i C_i^-1)`.

The `ArcMotion` quaternion swap went with it. It swapped y and z on every bone *except bone
0*, and that exemption was the tell: most bones carry an axis of `(90, 0, 0)`, where the
conjugation also moves the vector part between y and z, so the swap was a hand-fitted
stand-in for it. Bone 0 is the root, the one bone whose axis is zero and which therefore
needed no stand-in. The two had to change together — which is why removing the swap alone
had previously scored 166 degrees out.

## Measured

Rig 18, `18000010`, against client video:

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| spine off vertical | +13.3° | −3.6° |
| head off vertical | +13.2° | −3.7° |
| lowest wing tip | 87% of stature above the ankle | 28% |

**The check that is not a fit:** the same math has to fold Aracoix wings and leave the
Nephilim's spread — a separate observation from client video. It does, with nothing aimed at
either rig: rig 18 tucks to a 0.61 lateral span while 115/116 keep 3.40.

Not chosen by score. A 192-variant sweep over component orders and signs *does* contain
variants that beat scalar-first on the two anchored angles; they are overfitting three
degrees of freedom against two numbers, and the best of them aggregate at 53. Scalar-first is
what `Math.dll` says the bytes are.

## What it moved downstream

Anything that composed clip rotations itself, or was calibrated off a posed model:

- **`tools/export_motions.py`** ran its own forward-kinematics loop and needed the same
  conjugation.
- **`AssetManager._fold_wings`** authors rotations that are fed *back* through `pose()`, so
  it now authors through `clip_rotation` to land in the frame `pose()` reads them in.
- **The exported motion tracks.** The bundle's contract is *apply track bones to nodes by
  name*; joint-frame quaternions make that false unless every consumer conjugates. The
  conjugation is baked into `export_motion_tracks.py` instead, so the contract stays
  literally true. Verified end to end: replaying the tracks by name with no rig lookup
  reproduces `ArcSkeleton.pose()` to 1.1e-3 over seven rigs and 1,384 joints, which is the
  4-decimal rounding the exporter documents.
- **`units_per_metre`, 2.7411 → 2.5994.** Below.

## The scale, and the toes again

The metre scale is derived from the Human model's bounding-box height, and that height was
being measured off a pose that leaned backwards and stood on its toes.

**The toes are the mechanism, and this is the second time they have come up** — `081026.md`
recorded `stand_pose` standing the foot up from the cache's toes-down bind, at 90.155°, and
Aegisfall's loader doing the same job a second time. Here they cost a calibration: the rest
pose runs every limb straight down its axis, so `LFOOT` reads `(0, -1, 0)`, en pointe. Posed
correctly it lies flat, `(0.16, -0.01, 0.99)`, and the bottom of the box rises 0.310 units —
the whole of the Human's 0.255 drop. A person's height is measured flat-footed, so the new
figure is the better yardstick.

Telling detail: **the old published Human height, 4.934, is exactly the rest-pose height**,
and the Dwarf's 4.055 likewise. Whatever the old stance was doing, it was not moving the
extremes of the box it was measured from.

**The Minotaur is the control.** It is the one playable rig whose stance comes from the rest
pose rather than a clip, so the joint frame cannot reach it — 6.521 before and after,
unchanged to three decimals. That is what says the rest geometry is untouched and every other
move came from applying clips correctly. The Aracoix is the loudest: 8.685 → 4.649, wings
down off the top of its own bounding box.

The furniture is the independent check and it backs the new scale, though not by much: a
Table reads 0.77 m against a 0.75 m standard, where the old scale gave 0.73.

Consequence: `export_rules.py` and `export_terrain.py` now read the scale out of
`reference/summary.json` at run time and record which source they used, rather than pinning
a constant that has now moved twice.

## Four exports that were sitting in plain sight

Once the pose was right, the gaps in the export were the interesting thing:

- **`animations/overrides.json`** — `Effects.cfg`'s `AnimOverride`, 1,345 rows over 243
  effects, was the one ANIMID source nothing read. **33 of its 39 target ANIMIDs are named by
  no other source**, including the whole 400–420 run: 714 (skeleton, ANIMID) pairs that were
  exported and unreachable. A row is `[source, *targets]` — 156 lines name two replacements
  for one slot, and reading a fixed pair silently cost ANIMIDs 322 and 324.
- **`content/powers.json` / `effects.json`** — all 1,465 powers and 2,950 effects, against the
  ~460 the wiki lists. Completeness is checked against the source and the check found two
  silent losses before it shipped: repeated `DESCRIPTION` keys were being assigned rather
  than accumulated (190 dropped), and effect-level flags sit outside every sub-block so all
  748 were being skipped — including every `IsItemEffect`, the field that explains why an
  effect has no power.
- **`content/rules.json`** — speeds, recovery, mod types, skills, and the curve table.
  `CompoundCurves.cfg` defines the `SL0083Up` tokens that `powers.json` could only quote, so
  the combat numbers become computable. 232 tokens used, all 232 resolve.
- **`maps/terrain/`** — the README said "14 continent heightmaps, not yet extracted". It is
  **178, all resolving**, plus the `terrain_gen` transform that turns an image into terrain.

## The wiki checks, and what a passing check was worth

Six comparisons against the Morloch wiki. Three found real defects, three found nothing,
and the ratio is less interesting than what the passing ones turned out to prove.

`items.json` shipped `damage` as `[null, null]` on all 4,021 rows — the exporter read
`item_min_damage` / `item_max_damage`, fields no item has, while the numbers sat in a
nested `item_weapon` record. **Nothing internal could have caught it.** An always-null
column is perfectly self-consistent and passes every completeness check the repo has,
because those checks verify the source was fully consumed, not that the right keys were
asked for. It took an outside source quoting `8 - 34` for a weapon we had nothing for.

The disciplines check found the worse one. Requirements are stored as
`{restrict, races|classes}` and the exporter read the list while ignoring the flag, which
**inverts** it. So a discipline restricted *away from* three races was published as
available to *only* those three: not incomplete, reversed. 619 items had their race list
inverted, 56 their class list, and 3,177 more shipped `[]` for "unrestricted" — which reads
exactly as naturally as "nobody may", and would have locked players out of most of the item
table. `null` and `[]` now mean different things and both occur.

The part worth remembering is why it survived so long. `check_races_wiki.py` and
`check_classes_wiki.py` score 12/12, 60/60, 60/60, 167/167 and 31/31, 99/99, 69/69 — and
score **exactly that on the broken export and the fixed one alike**. 26 of the 27 class
runes store `restrict: false`, the one case where the naive read and the correct read
agree. Two perfect scores, reported honestly, over a field that was backwards on 675 rows.
Neither check was wrong; neither covered the case that was. A check that only exercises the
easy majority passes for the wrong reason, and I had been reading those two passes as
evidence about `content/` when they were only ever evidence about races and classes.

Three false alarms in the same run, all this side's parsing: `all races` as prose,
`Shades` for `Shade`, `Skydancer` for `Sky Dancer`. And one that looked like a fourth and
was not — Drannok requires base class **Fighter** and race **Vampire**, where the wiki
lists Warlock and Warrior. The classes promoting from Fighter that a Vampire may take are
exactly those two. The cache states the rule, the wiki enumerates what satisfies it;
`promotes_into` resolves it in code now rather than asserting it in a comment.

So the same run produced a real defect and four false alarms. The lesson is not that low
scores are usually noise, nor that they are usually real. The score is not the finding.

The seventh check, against `Starting Traits`, found the same failure mode as `damage` but
larger: not a wrong column, six **absent** ones. `item_attr_req`, `rune_sub_type`,
`item_user_power_action`, `item_power_grant`, `rune_rank` and
`rune_is_standard_character_creation` were all in the cache and none reached the export.

The last of those is the one that mattered. `talents.json` had 240 rows and no way to tell
that only 92 are player-selectable — the other 148 are NPC runes (`Archer Mob`, `Belgosch
Lord`) in the same table. A creation screen built from that file offers `Anti-Tank Boss
Mob` next to `Agile`. No internal check could see it: every row was complete, consistent,
and correctly parsed. The wiki documenting exactly 85 of them is the only reason the
question got asked, and again the check that would have caught it is the one nobody had a
reason to write.

Two joins came out of following it. `applies_effects` names a *power action*, not an
effect, and most action ids coincide with the effect they apply — which is why 212 of 231
tokens resolved anyway and the 19 that did not looked like noise. `TRT-TIRELESS` applies
`TIRELESS`. `export_powers.py` had parsed the whole action table all along and emitted only
the per-power slice, so the actions no power lists were unreachable. Emitting the table
takes the rune join to 231/231.

The other is not ours to fix: 194 effects carry a placeholder where the display name goes.
`MOVE-B-5% "MOB" 0` and `RES-MAGIC-B-5 "TALENT" 0` are what `Effects.cfg` literally says.
The parse was right; the field is junk in the shipped data, and an effect's meaning has to
be read from its `mods`.

407 of 408 fields agree. The one that does not is the wiki giving `Blood of the Dragon` a
movement bonus the COBJECT does not carry — checked against the raw record, not the export,
because the whole point of the previous paragraphs is that "our export says so" is not
evidence about the cache.

Two smaller things worth recording. Five talent names carry **two rows each** with
different data, so keying that file by name silently drops one; my first run did exactly
that and reported `Wizard's Apprentice` as four disagreements against a row the wiki was
never describing. And the 23 race requirements on this page agree exactly, which is an
independent confirmation of the `restrict` fix from a page that had no part in finding it.

## The structures check I had already declared impossible

I wrote in the guide that the wiki could not check `structures.json`: of 250 buildable
names, 7 exist as wiki pages, one carries a rank table, so there was nothing to compare
against. Every number in that sentence is correct and the conclusion was wrong. The
buildings are not pages. They are **52 `=== sections ===` of a single `Buildings` page**,
grouped under five architecture headings, each with Cost, Hirelings, Extras and Size.

So the same mistake twice in one pass, in opposite directions. With races and classes I
read two passing checks as evidence about `content/` when they only covered races and
classes. Here I read one failing search as evidence about the wiki when it only covered
page titles. Both times the check did not cover the thing, and both times I let it speak
about the thing anyway. A search that finds nothing is a fact about the search.

Once the page was actually read the join needed three naming rules, all mechanical: the
wiki drops the `Feudal ` prefix because Feudal is the default style, spells `Invorii` for
`Invorri`, and writes `Cottage [Log, Stone, Wood]` where the cache has three rows. Four
more sections are titled by function, and in each case the wiki's own description settles
it -- `War Temple` says "the militant philosophy of the Temple of the Cleansing Flame",
which is a structure name in the cache. All 52 match.

The sweep that followed found the same shape as the talents one: the export was reading
**5 of about 60 fields** on these records. `template_architecture` -- the very thing the
wiki groups its list by -- was among the dropped, as were the city influence and no-build
radii, the NPC slots, and floors, levels and doors on the structure half.

102 of 107 fields agree, and all five disagreements are walls. The Irekei wall templates
are typed `Feudal` where the Elven and Invorri ones are typed correctly, and no wall
template allows a hireling at any rank though the wiki garrisons all four families with
archers. Both are stated rather than absorbed; someone with a running server can settle
them, and I cannot.

Size as a grid footprint has no counterpart in the cache -- it is not the same quantity as
the radii the template stores, and inventing a mapping from radius to footprint would have
manufactured agreement instead of testing for it. I also wrote that Cost was server-side.
That one was wrong, and the next section says how.

## Going to fix the Irekei walls, and finding out what the field means

The Irekei wall finding above was written as "typed Feudal where its siblings are typed
correctly", and the obvious next move was to correct the three rows. Establishing that they
were wrong is what stopped it.

`sb.exe` has `ArcCityAssetTemplate::ParseKeyword: ARCHITECTURE=`, which is the field. It
also has `ArcGameZone::ParseKeyword: ARCHITECTURE=` -- **zones carry the same field** --
and the message `PlaceError:ArchitectureCannotPlaceInZone`, "This city architecture cannot
be placed in this zone." Reading the 861 zone records confirms it: `Tyrranth Major` is
`["Feudal", "Irekei", "Elven"]`, `Uthgaard` is `["Elven"]`, and 837 zones carry nothing.

So `template_architecture` is **not a style label**. It is the set of zone architectures
that will accept the asset. For an ordinary building those coincide, which is why grouping
by it agrees with the wiki 43 times in 44 -- but the coincidence is doing the work, not the
semantics. The tell was there in the data before I went to the binary and I had read past
it: the 9 templates with more than one entry are all trainer halls, and `Elven Guild Hall`
is `["Feudal", "Forest", "Mountains"]`. An *Elven* hall, not tagged Elven, tagged with two
biomes. That is a placement filter wearing a style's name, and I had described the column
in the guide as "how the wiki groups its list".

Which leaves the three Irekei walls as a genuine inconsistency with a much smaller
consequence than "mis-styled": they are offered under the Feudal list rather than the
Irekei one, and since nearly every playable zone permits Feudal they remain placeable
everywhere they should be. The evidence that it is unintended is still good -- 2000195 and
2000196, the Irekei towers, sit in the same contiguous id block with the same dual-name
shape and are tagged `Irekei` -- but "inconsistent with its neighbours" is not "wrong", and
this export's whole value is being what shipped. Left alone, documented, with the reasoning
recorded so the next person does not have to redo it.

What did get fixed is the half that was actually missing: `zones/index.json` now carries
`architecture`, so the field can be evaluated at all. Exporting a placement filter without
the thing it filters against is not much better than not exporting it.

One more, found by accident while chasing this. I had written that building costs are
server-side, on the grounds that `items.json` holds three generic deed rows. True of
`items.json`, false of the cache: **deeds are a separate asset kind and there are 880 of
them**, each carrying `item_value` -- `Outer Wall Gatehouse Deed` 80,000 -- plus
`deed_structure_id`, `deed_start_rank` and `deed_employment`. `export_content.py` never
reads `AssetKind.DEED`. So Cost is checkable against the wiki, and the guide now says so
instead of calling it an absence. Same failure as everything else in this log: the search
covered `items.json` and I let it speak about the cache.

## The deeds, and the cleanest result of the whole pass

`content/deeds.json`, 880 rows. The cost column agrees with the wiki **52 times out of 52**
-- every price on the `Buildings` page, from the 1,500,000 Citadel down to the 50,000 wall
sets.

That is the strongest single result of any of these comparisons, and worth being precise
about why. The prices live in a *different asset kind* from the buildings they place. The
join runs deed name to wiki section title, with no id in common, across two sources sharing
no lineage -- a player-written table on one side, a binary object archive on the other.
Fifty-two independent numbers matching exactly is not something a decoding error can fake.
It also strengthens the building join retroactively: the deeds use the wiki's own bare
spelling -- `Barracks Deed`, not `Feudal Barracks Deed` -- which is a fair sign the page
was written off the builder's list rather than from the world.

`target_id` needed care, and is the one place I nearly repeated the morning's mistake in
miniature. It resolves against the asset catalogue on 380 deeds and *looks* like it
resolves on 869, because ids below 10,000 are a **server-side table index** -- `Feudal
Outer Walls` is 1, `Trebuchet` 10, `Healer Trainer` 103 -- and those collide with genuine
low-numbered props and creatures. Taking the catalogue's word would have shipped 489
confident, wrong joins, every one internally consistent and unfalsifiable from inside.
`target_kind` is null wherever the id is out of asset space: the honest answer rather than
the complete-looking one.

Four wiki columns have now been checked against sources this export had not touched at the
start of the pass -- requirements through the `restrict` flag, talents through `Starting
Traits`, buildings through `Buildings`, prices through an asset kind nobody had opened.
Every one of them was reachable the whole time.

## The wall hirelings, which were never missing

I reported twice that no wall template allows a hireling at any rank, and offered it as a
thing a running server would have to settle. It did not need a server. It needed the other
field.

`ranks[].hirelings` is a per-rank count. `valid_npc_categories` is which NPC classes may be
stationed. They are different quantities and neither implies the other -- the towers read
`0` for the first and `[27]` for the second, while `Snowy Orc Tower` reads `1-4` for the
first and nothing for the second. I checked the count, found zeros, and wrote up an absence.

Category **27** turns out to be the wall-tower and gatehouse slot: `Concave Tower`, `Convex
Tower`, `Outer Wall with Tower`, `Gate House`, `Small Gate House`, `Stockade Tower`, in all
four architectures. Category **28** is `Artillery Tower`, again in all four. Between them
they are precisely the wiki's `Archer Captain, Wall Archer, Tower Artillery Captain`. The
archers go in the towers, which is where anyone would put them, and the plain wall segments
take nobody -- which is also what you would expect and is exactly what the data said.

Reading it correctly meant widening the wall family to include towers, which is what the
wiki's section describes anyway: it prices the set as one 50k system and garrisons it with
a Tower Artillery Captain. **That widening also took architecture from 43/44 to 44/44**,
because the Irekei towers are tagged `Irekei` and now sit inside the section. I want that
recorded rather than banked: the underlying oddity has not changed. 2000192/3/4 still carry
`["Feudal"]` where their Elven and Invorri counterparts carry their own style, and anyone
grouping buildings by that field will still find three Irekei wall pieces under Feudal.
Nothing in the export was edited to produce the agreement -- only the question was asked
correctly.

159 of 159 now. Which is a good score and a slightly uncomfortable one, because two of
those fields were reported as cache defects by me, in writing, in a merged commit, on
evidence that was never sufficient. The pattern this whole log keeps circling is not that
the data is subtle. It is that a confident negative -- "the wiki has nothing", "the cache
has nothing", "no template allows this" -- is the cheapest thing in the world to write and
the most expensive thing to leave standing.

## Overriding the Irekei tags, and keeping the check honest about it

Asked for twice, so done: `ARCHITECTURE_CORRECTIONS` in `export_content.py` rewrites
templates 2000192/3/4 from `["Feudal"]` to `["Irekei"]`. It is the only place this export
departs from the cache, and it is worth setting out what made it defensible in the end,
because I declined it once.

What changed my footing was the structure id block. Those three templates build ids
1309200-1310000, which is one contiguous run whose other members are named `Irekei Outer
Wall Gate`, `Irekei Outer Wall with Stairs` and `Irekei Outer Straight Wall`. The
unprefixed names *inside that run* -- `Outer Wall Gate` at 1309200 and 1309400 -- are
Irekei assets nobody renamed, not Feudal ones. Add the towers at 2000195/6 tagged `Irekei`
in the same template block, and the wiki listing them as ordinary Irekei buildings, and
three independent lines of evidence point the same way. That is a different standard of
proof from "it looks inconsistent", which is all I had the first time.

The caps stay untouched, and the reason is the same reason the walls moved: caps are
Feudal in *every* style, including 2000207/2000208, which build structures literally named
`Elven Outer Wall Cap`. Uniform is a rule, not an anomaly. An override that also swept up
the caps would have been me tidying the data to taste.

Two things keep it auditable. `architecture_shipped` carries the cache's original on
exactly those three rows and nowhere else, so nothing is destroyed and a diff shows the
departure. And `check_structures_wiki.py` now scores architecture against
`architecture_shipped` rather than the corrected value, and prints the overridden rows
above its table.

That second one matters more than it looks. Without it the checker would be reading a
correction I made *because of the wiki* and reporting that it agrees *with the wiki* --
a loop that manufactures a perfect score out of an edit. Every other number in this log
would have been slightly cheapened by sitting next to one. The 44/44 still describes the
client's own data; the override sits beside it, labelled.

## Sweeping the props and items, and finding the armour has no armour

The template sweep had worked twice, so I ran it against the remaining asset kinds.

**PROP, LIGHT and OTHER have nothing**, and that is a real result rather than an empty one.
All 1,575 props populate 14 fields between them and every single one is engine plumbing --
render object, scale, cull distance, collision, sound. There is no content hiding in them.
What a prop is *for* lives in `zones/`, which places it, and `models/`, which draws it.

**ITEM is a different story: 41 populated fields the exporter never named.** Most are
plumbing too, but buried in them was this. 2,361 rows are ARMOR. Not one carried a
protection value. `item_defense_rating` sits on 1,574 of them -- a Robe of the Crimson
Circle is 110 -- and nothing read it.

That is the `damage` defect exactly, on the other half of the equipment table, five months
of exports later. A weapon table with no damage and an armour table with no armour are the
same bug. Only the first was ever found, and the reason is uncomfortable: the wiki check I
wrote to catch the first one fetched `Category:Weapons` and stopped there. The tool built
in response to the bug was scoped so it could not find the bug's twin.

`durability` is the same story in miniature and is almost funny. `parse_weapon` has pulled
`Durability:` off the wiki since the day that file was written. It was never in the
comparison loop -- because `items.json` had no `durability` column for it to be compared
against. So every run read the number, dropped it, and reported nothing, and the absence of
a `durability` row in the output table read as "the wiki does not state it". It compares on
221 weapons and 49 armour pieces now.

Nine other columns came out with the defense rating, and two are worth calling out. **1,030
items are sex-restricted** -- 942 female, 88 male -- which is an equip constraint that
simply did not exist in the export. And **1,380 armour pieces carry a separate female
mesh**; binding the male one to a female skeleton is a visible rendering error, and a
consumer had no way to know the alternative existed.

Armour now checks against `Category:Armor`: 751 wiki rows, 49 matching by name, defense
33/49, durability 35/49, weight 37/49 exact, with the remainder scattered -- the same
patch-drift signature the weapons show, not a decoding error.

Three times in one file, then, the same shape: **the check only ever asked about the
columns the export happened to have.** An export and its own validator, written by the same
hand, share a blind spot by construction. The wiki was never what found these -- it was the
thing that made a gap visible once someone pointed the comparison at a column that was not
already there.

## Sweeping the config archives

147 distinct `.cfg` files ship, 203 counting the 57 per-server copies of
`Guild_Restrictions`. Seven are read. The great majority of the rest is client presentation
-- 40 `SCREEN_*`, 34 `Transitions/`, 17 weather definitions, sound tables, credits, the
profanity list -- and has no mechanical content at all. Four hold mechanics and are now
catalogued in the guide rather than left to be rediscovered: `PowActionCostInfo.cfg` (the
reagent cost table, 47 KB of `PRE-003 "Iron" 2 "Sulfur" 1`), `StartingKitTable.cfg`, the
`Ranks_*` guild titles, and the guild government configs.

Two were worth exporting immediately.

**`CharCreateRuneList.cfg` is the client's own list of what character creation offers**,
and it is a stricter statement than the `standard_creation` flag I had been trusting. All
84 of its trait runes are inside the 92 that flag carries. The eight it leaves out are
`Wolfpack Developer`, `QA Test Rune`, `Proficient with Bows`, and **the higher id of all
five duplicated talent names**.

Two things fall out of that. The first is that anything filtering on `standard_creation`
was offering players a QA rune. The second is better: this file offers 250080 and never
250123 for `Proficient with Axes`, which is the same row the wiki describes, reached
independently. When I found those duplicates I reasoned from the wiki that the lower id was
the real one; the client's own creation list says so too, from a completely different
direction. That is the first time in this pass two independent sources have confirmed a
judgement call rather than one source correcting a mistake.

**And the zone-to-continent question was never open.** I have been listing it as needing a
running server since the start of this log. The guide's own words were that
`ZoneDataENGLISH.cfg` "does not place a zone on a continent". The file does not -- but
**there are three of them**, in `AerynthIcons/`, `DalgothIcons/` and `VorringiaIcons/`, and
the directory is the answer. 129 of 135 names sit under exactly one continent; the six
under all three are the battlegrounds and Chaos zones, which exist on every world.

That is the fifth time in this pass, and it is the same sentence every time: a statement
about what I looked at, written down as a statement about what exists. `zones/macrozones.json`
carries it now, with the level bands, and 25 of the 135 join to a zone record here -- the
rest are Dalgoth and Vorringia zones this Aerynth cache simply does not contain, which the
absence of any near miss among 729 named zones confirms.

## The enchantment costs, and what ITEM-A turned out to be

`PowActionCostInfo.cfg` was the largest of the four mechanical configs left uncatalogued,
and it exports cleanly: 524 item prefixes and suffixes with their reagent cost, now
`content/enchantments.json`. **Everything joins.** All 524 ids are power actions in the
`actions` table -- the one emitted three commits ago for an unrelated reason -- and all 17
reagents are rows in `items.json`. Nothing had to be guessed.

The chain is cost -> action -> effects -> mods, and following it answered a question I had
written off as unanswerable. When the talents check ran into 194 effects carrying a
placeholder where a display name belongs, I recorded that `Effects.cfg` ships
`MOVE-B-5% "MOB" 0`, concluded the field was junk in the shipped data, and moved on. That
was right about the field and wrong about the effects. **`ITEM-A` and `ITEM-B` are 1,132 of
those 2,950 effects, and they are the two halves of an item enchantment** -- A carrying the
name, the gold value and the primary mod, B the secondary. Not junk at all; a naming
convention I had no key for until the cost file supplied one.

The nicest part is a check that costs nothing. The display name is in the cache twice --
as the `#` comment in the cost file, and as an `ItemName` mod on the effect -- written by
different hands into different files. **521 of 524 agree exactly and none disagrees.** Both
are exported rather than one being collapsed into the other, so if a future cache breaks
that agreement it will be visible instead of averaged away.

The three exceptions are a real defect, in the client rather than here: `PRE-335 Hardened`,
`PRE-336 Forged` and `PRE-337 Trueforged` each apply an effect id -- `PRE-335A` and
siblings -- that `Effects.cfg` does not define. `read_actions` only records ids it can
resolve, so those came out with an empty effect list, which reads exactly like "this
enchantment does nothing". They now carry `missingEffects` instead. An empty list that
means "broken reference" and an empty list that means "no effect" being the same value is
the `[]` versus `null` problem from the `restrict` flag, in a new place.

## The starting kits, and four files agreeing

`StartingKitTable.cfg` -> `content/starting_kits.json`: 68 race/sex/class blocks, 451
loadouts. All 46 item ids resolve, every loadout is labelled, nothing is left over.

One decision worth recording. The file's own header says the columns are `Legs Torso Feet
Hand Weapon Inv1 Inv2 Inv3`, and most rows honour it -- but a `Bow` row puts the bow in the
fourth column and leaves the rest blank. Trusting the header would have silently filed a
bow as a glove on some unknown number of rows, and the failure would have looked exactly
like correct data. So the export resolves each id and reads the slot off the *item*, which
is already in `items.json` and cannot drift out of step. Positional parsing of a file that
is only mostly positional is the same class of mistake as the AnimOverride fixed-pair read
that cost two ANIMIDs, and I would rather not make it twice.

What makes this one satisfying is not the file. It is that four independently written
sources now agree about character creation:

  * `CharCreateRuneList.cfg` offers 22 race/sex combinations.
  * `StartingKitTable.cfg` has a kit for 22 race/sex combinations.
  * They are **the same 22**, with nothing on either side unmatched.
  * All 68 kit blocks are for a class that `races.json`'s cross-link says that race may
    take -- and that cross-link is built from the `restrict` flag, the thing that was
    backwards on 675 rows this morning.

That last line is the one I would keep. The restrict fix was validated against the wiki,
which is a player-written secondary source. Here it is confirmed a second time by a config
file that has nothing to do with the wiki and nothing to do with the rune records the fix
was applied to. Three sources, no shared lineage, same answer.

Which is the opposite of the pattern that has run through this entire log. Every defect
found today was invisible because one source was being asked one question. Every
confirmation worth having came from a second source asking the same question a different
way.
