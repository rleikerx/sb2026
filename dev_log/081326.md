# 13 Aug 2026 — the wings nobody had looked at

The Nephilim had been shipping its bind pose since the export began, and the number that
should have said so was being read as a fact about the race.

## What was wrong

Its wings stood over its head in a vertical fan of spars. `reference/summary.json` gave the
Nephilim **8.260 units**, 1.77× a Human, against the Aracoix's 1.08× on the same body plan
— and that figure had been sitting in the per-race table being used as a stature.

The cause needed no new data. The rig's own idle clip, `115000010`, drives 40 bones
including all twelve wing channels. The stand pose being shipped covered 28 bones and none
of them, because `_calmest_frame` takes the standing frame that *disturbs the rest pose
least* and a clip that never mentions the wings disturbs it less than one that sweeps them
out to where a wing belongs. The gate was written to reject frames standing with their arms
in the air. It also selects against wings, and the more of a creature a clip animates the
more the rule holds against it.

## What found it

A person looking at a render, again. `tools/pose_sheet.py` had been drawing the fan for as
long as the defect existed; nobody had drawn the Nephilim.

Two things then settled the shape, neither of them ours: `C:\code\Nephilm.mp4`, client
capture of the race standing in Aerynth, and the rig itself. Wings out to both sides,
membrane hanging, tops barely above the head — which is exactly what `115000010` draws when
you apply it.

**The reading it replaced was one of ours, and it had been recorded as a success.** From
`081126.md`: the joint frame "folds Aracoix wings and leaves the Nephilim's spread", 115/116
holding a 3.40 lateral span against rig 18's 0.61. The span is real. The *shape* was never
looked at, and a number that matched an expectation ended the question — the same failure
this log has recorded five times in other clothes, now in the one place that felt settled.

## The fix, and the wrong fix that came first

`AssetManager._wings_from_clip`. Choose the body frame exactly as before, then fill an
untouched wing subtree from the calmest standing frame that does drive it. Same gate, same
score, wing bones only. It is the mirror of `_fold_wings`, which *authors* rotations
because no frame in this cache folds a Griffon's wings; this reads a frame because for
these rigs one exists. Layer `upright+wingclip`: nothing on a Nephilim is invented.

The first attempt ranked frames on wing coverage inside `_calmest_frame`, which is the
obvious place and is wrong. It fixed the Nephilim and moved both Aracoix — because their
calmest frames carry no wing channels *either*, and `_fold_wings` had been supplying those
afterwards. Two rigs of collateral movement on a change whose entire claim was that it
touched one race. `pose_baseline.py` caught it in one run, which is the second time this
week that file has earned itself.

There was a third wrong version in between, worth a line: keying the wing test to the
*frame* rather than to the rig's own bones. Clips are shared across skeletons — skeleton 1's
token list names several of rig 18's — so the human rig began choosing its stance by whether
a clip animated wings it has never had.

## What the same question turned up next door

Three numbers in the export guide disagreed with the bundle shipping beside it: the Aracoix
(4.649 against 5.044), the Nephilim (4.430), and the Shade — where the table had asset 2016
instead of 2015, which is the `2011_Human` / `2012_Human` trap the same page documents four
paragraphs later. Fixed, and the twelve figures now come from `_scale_reference` rather than
from prose that was true once.

**Skeleton 12, the big-cat rig, is left broken on purpose.** Body prone and stretched, legs
splayed, head floating clear of the neck; a plain Cheetah does it exactly as the Chimera
does, and a Hunting Hound on skeleton 10 stands correctly from the same code. Depth over
height runs 3.5 across the rig against 2.05 for the hound. 80 assets carry it — every cat
in the bestiary, the three Chimeras, the summoned familiars. `_wings_from_clip` worked
because the Nephilim's own idle demonstrably holds the shape the client draws; nobody has
established what a standing frame for the cat rig looks like, and guessing would bake a
sprawl into 80 assets and call it a pose.

## Reviewing that last paragraph, four hours later

Asked to review the skeleton 12 notes, and the first finding is that there were none: every
word written about that rig anywhere in this repo was written above, this morning, by me.
Nothing predates it and nothing had checked it. Three of its claims do not survive contact.

**"Nobody has established what a standing frame looks like" implied the cache might not
hold one. It holds seven.** The rig has eight clips, and at its calmest accepted frame
seven of them draw a cat standing squarely on four legs — `12000001` f12, `12000010` f32,
`12000055` f28, `12000059` f2, `12000075` f10, `12000077` f15, `12000130` f22. The eighth,
`12000002` f6, is the sprawl. It ships because at calm **0.429** it beats every standing
frame in the rig, which score 0.55 to 0.79. `images/pose_review/catrig_frames.png` draws
all nine side by side, and no judgement call is needed to see it.

So this is the Nephilim defect a second time, in a body with no wings. `_calmest_frame`
measures distance from the bind pose; the bind pose runs every bone down one axis; a body
lying flat with its legs stretched out is *nearer to that* than one standing with its legs
bent under it. On a quadruped, calm is very nearly an anti-stand test. I wrote in the
STAND_WING_BONES comment that a wing's bind pose is not a resting shape — and did not
notice that morning that the sentence is about bind poses generally, not about wings.

**"It is the rig"** — skeleton 12 *is* skeleton 10, the hound's 44 bones under the same
names in the same order, plus TAIL5 and TAIL6. The rig is not what differs.

**Two gates that reject nothing.** Sampled across both rigs, every frame passes
`limbs-down` and `on-ground`: 7 of 7 on eight cat clips, 7 of 7 on eleven hound clips. The
hound stands because its calmest frame happens to be a stand, not because anything tested
for one. I had described a stance ladder failing to find a stand; there is no filtering
happening on that rung at all.

**And the number was inflated.** Depth over height is 3.56 on the shipped sprawl, but a
correctly standing cheetah on this rig reads 2.7 to 2.9, against the hound's 2.05. A big cat
is genuinely long. The sprawl adds about a quarter to it, not all of it, and "a cheetah 4.4
m long is the sprawl, not the animal" was the same rhetorical overshoot this log keeps
catching in other people's notes.

Left unfixed still, and now for the honest reason rather than the one I gave: the repair is
a change to how a stance is *chosen*, which decides the pose of every rig this export
ships, where `_wings_from_clip` could be confined to a step that runs after the body frame
is settled. That is a real difference in blast radius. It is not a shortage of evidence,
and the note should not have implied one.

## The check that would have caught it

Bones in the rig against bones the stand pose covers. 15 unposed is normal on a 43-bone
humanoid — fingers, sheaths, helm, beard, which no clip drives — and the Aracoix carried the
same 15 on 55 bones. The Nephilim carried 27: those 15 plus its twelve wing bones exactly.
One number, computable from the cache without rendering anything, that says which rigs are
wearing their bind pose in public. It is in the guide now. Two rigs still read high, 15 and
91, and both are the `rest` case — no clip stands either body at all — and neither is bound
to a creature in this build.
