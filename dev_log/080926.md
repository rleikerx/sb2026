# 08/09/26

## Summary

Built a headless renderer and a rest-vs-posed contact sheet so creature pose work can
be checked by looking at it, then used them to fix the biped pose ladder.

**`tools/pose_render.py`** — a pure numpy z-buffer rasteriser over whatever
`AssetCatalog.assemble()` produces. No GPU, no Qt, no display, no new dependency:
numpy and Pillow only, both already required. It drives the real assembler rather
than reimplementing it. Orthographic side and front views, flat two-sided lambert,
a line marking the y=0 plane the skeleton root stands on, and parts tinted by limb
class with the left side drawn darker. The tint is a diagnostic, not decoration: in
flat grey an arm in the air and a leg in the air look identical, and a mirroring
fault is invisible without a side cue.

**`tools/pose_sheet.py`** — rest pose on the left, posed on the right, same camera
box for both, labelled with asset id, skeleton id and which rung of `stand_pose`
answered. Writes to `images/pose_review/`, which is tracked, so a pose change leaves
a picture somebody else can disagree with instead of a claim that it looked fine.

**Two defects fixed in `AssetManager.stand_pose`.**

*The upright gate was judging bipeds on a sixth of the evidence.* It probed frame 0
of each clip (`samples=1`) where every other rung probes six. Two rigs — Hungry Dead
(16) and Marsh Crone (49) — had frames passing the existing gate untouched and were
sitting at rest because nobody had looked past the first frame of their clips. A clip
does not put its calmest frame first.

*A biped that cannot straighten its legs had no rung at all.* `_stands_hunched`
allows leg tilt to 55° — but only alongside the three things a sprawl gets wrong:
feet still flat, body still taller than it is wide, feet still the lowest part of it.
Widening the tilt threshold on its own was already known to re-admit sprawl, and it
does; leg tilt alone cannot separate a crouch from a body lying down with its knees
up. Anything from 55° to 65° selects the same eight skeletons, so the bound sits in a
gap rather than on a cliff edge.

**Both are layered as fallbacks, so coverage cannot decrease**, and it did not:
nothing lost a pose it already had.

| layer | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| upright | 19 skeletons / 873 assets | 22 / 910 |
| hunched | — | 8 / 401 |
| grounded | 48 / 894 | 47 / 892 |
| rest | 18 / 613 | **8 / 177** |

**The first version of the hunched rung was wrong and the contact sheet is what
caught it** — 5 of 11 newly posed rigs were visibly bad: Grobones with its arms out,
Cave Troll mid-swing, Bearkin flung forward, Wight and Ice Fiend mid-stride. Both
causes were things the stance gates do not ask about. A walk frame answers every
question about legs and feet correctly, so `_feet_together` asks whether the feet are
a stride apart (measured: stands sit at 0.09–0.33 of body height, walks at
0.46–0.52). And `STAND_MAX_ARM_RISE` at +0.35 was measured on quadruped forelimbs,
where it means a settled dog; on two legs the same value is a figure reaching out in
front of itself, so `STAND_MAX_BIPED_ARM_RISE` is 0.0 — a standing biped does not
hold a forelimb above horizontal (measured: good poses −0.82 to −0.06, bad ones +0.35
to +0.58). Both are applied through `_stance_ladder`, three rungs strictest first,
because some rigs have frames with the arms down and none with the feet also
together, and one conjunction drops those rigs back to a swinging arm to buy a stride
they were never going to lose.

Second pass: **8 of 11 read correctly by eye**, up from 2.

`stand_pose` now records which rung answered (`stand_layer`), so the sheet reports
the choice rather than re-deriving it. A reporting tool that re-ran the gates itself
would drift the first time a rung moved, and the label would look right while being
wrong.

Invariants checked, not assumed: `--rest-pose` **byte-identical** on all six probe
ids (2000, 2001, 12000, 24000, 124100, 1000, sha256 compared before and after);
manifest **9924 rows across 6 kinds** after re-exporting all 2,380 creatures;
coverage never decreased.

crossed before.** Every pose it has ever produced was *selected* — a frame the
client's own motion cache holds, picked by the stance ladder. The wing rotations
on four skeletons are **authored**: written here, by us, on the project owner's

**Exactly what is authored, so that nobody has to guess later:**

| | |
|---|---|
| skeletons | **18** and **20** (Aracoix), **70** (Hunting Griffon), **117** (Nelchael) |
| bones | the subtree under `LWINGJOINT`/`RWINGJOINT` (`LSHOULDERJB`/`RSHOULDERJ_B` on Nelchael), **excluding those root joints themselves** — 10 bones on 18/20/117, 12 on 70 |
| assets | **73 of the 2,380 exported creatures** |
| everything else | untouched: the body keeps the frame the ladder chose, and the root joints keep whatever that frame gave them |

`AssetManager.stand_layer` reports **`+wingfold`** for exactly these rigs, so the
contact sheets and every future report say out loud which poses are not wholly
the cache's. `13465`, `2002`, `2003` and `14078` are in `PROBE_ASSETS` for the
same reason — an authored pose is the one place a picture is the only evidence
there is.

**The rule is one sentence, and one constant.** *Every bone in the wing points
backward and `WING_FOLD_PITCH_DEG` (10 degrees) below horizontal.* Nothing else.

The **shape** is still the cache's: laying every wing bone along one direction is
what the source's own closed-wing frames do — the Griffon's clips rotate
`WING03/04/05` about X until all three point the same way. Only the *direction*
is ours.

**The direction is stated in model space, not in the torso's frame, and that is
the whole of why one constant serves three rigs.** Measured in the torso's frame
the Griffon wants 180 degrees (along its spine, which is horizontal) and the
Aracoix wants 120 or less (past that its wings go through the floor), and no
single value works. In model space those are the *same answer*, because a folded
wing lies back along the ground the creature stands on rather than along its own
spine. It rests on the rig facing +Z, which is this format's convention and was
checked rather than assumed: the torso's yaw in the chosen frame is **0.6 degrees
or less on all four rigs**.

**The 10 degrees was picked by rendering, not by scoring.** How far the wing tips
end up below the lowest part of the body, in body heights:

| pitch | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Griffon (70) | +0.884 | **+0.333** | -0.203 | -0.705 |
| Aracoix (18) | +0.889 | **+0.665** | +0.447 | +0.243 |

so the Griffon puts its wings through the ground past about 15 degrees. At 0 the
*mesh* clears the crown of the back by so little that the wingtips read as two
spikes above the head — which the bone-tip figures do not show and a picture
does. 10 is the value where neither happens on any of the three.

## The mirror, and why no bone name is read for its side

A wing pair is one mesh reused per side (`flip`), so a rule that took the side
from a name prefix would be exactly the trap the brief warned about. Nothing here
reads a name for a side. Each bone is turned by the **shortest rotation from its
own rest direction onto the target**, and because the target lies in the mirror
plane, reflecting the argument reflects the answer — the two sides come out as
each other's mirror by construction rather than by a branch. The antipodal
fallback is a half turn about X, which is its own mirror, and X is always
perpendicular to the target because the fold direction has no lateral part.

Verified end to end rather than argued: **5 left and 5 right wing bones (3 and 3
on Nelchael) land on the fold direction to within 7e-16.** A mirror bug would
have put half of them somewhere else.

The drawn wings are *not* perfectly symmetric, and that is the body, not the
fold: mirror residual **1.79 -> 1.65 x10^-2** world units on skeleton 18 and
**1.95 -> 1.51** on Nelchael, whose torso is rolled 39 degrees so that both its
shoulders sit on the same side of the centre line. The fold **reduces** it on all
four.

# 08/09/26 — third run

## Summary

Nelchael's wings were folded onto a body bent forty degrees sideways. **Selection
fixed it** — no rotation is authored here, and the second run's authored wing fold
remains the only invented data in this exporter.

**What was actually wrong, because the previous two entries had it wrong.** Both
recorded skeleton 117 as having *"no arms-down frame at all"* and filed it as a data
limit. It has **47**. Of the 430 frames across its 12 clips, 47 pass the upright
gate, and **all 47** have its arms hanging and its feet together. What it has none of
is a frame where its *second* pair of arms also hangs — and those are its wings,
whose rotations `_fold_wings` throws away and rewrites. `STAND_ARM_BONES` is
`('HUMERUS', 'RADIUS')` and this rig spells its wing bones `LHUMERUS_B`,
`LRADIUS_B`, `LRADIUS_BB`, so the arm test was being answered by bones that never
reach the output. **13 skeletons carry fold-target bones and only this one names
them as arms**; every other winged rig spells them `LWING01`.

So both preference rungs were unsatisfiable and the rig fell to the bottom rung,
which was **unconditioned** — it took whatever stood, in whatever attitude. That is
the real defect, and it is not about wings at all: **nothing in the ladder had ever
asked where the body was.** Every gate on that page is about legs, feet or arms, so
a frame can put both soles flat, both legs under the body and both arms down while
the torso is rolled onto one shoulder, and pass all of them.

Measured on the frame the ladder chose (clip 117000075 frame 3): legs tilted **28.6
degrees** against a 30 bound, soles **3.2 degrees** off flat against 10, feet
**0.245** body-heights apart against 0.35 — comfortably inside every threshold this
project has — and the spine **39.4 degrees off vertical**. It is a *roll*, not a
bow: decomposed, the sideways part is 39.4 and the forward part is **0.0**.

## The fix is one rung and one constant

`gate + torso upright`, inserted between the arms rung and the bare gate.
`STAND_TORSO_BONES = ('UPPERBACK', 'LOWERBACK')`, `STAND_MAX_TORSO_LEAN = 30.0`,
worst of the spine bones — the convention `_stance_angles` already uses for legs and
feet. It picks **clip 117000131 frame 30**.

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| spine off vertical (UPPERBACK) | **39.4 deg** | **0.7 deg** |
| worst spine bone | 39.4 | 5.0 |
| head centroid, sideways from the feet | **-0.834** | **+0.168** |
| chest mesh axis off vertical | **40.8 deg** | **11.4 deg** |
| leg tilt / sole roll / foot gap | 28.6 / 3.2 / 0.245 | 29.2 / 3.4 / 0.217 |
| primary arm rise | -0.48 | **-0.92** |
| drawn height | 5.30 | 5.70 |

The chest figure is the one that settles it: **11.4 degrees is what that mesh reads
in the rest pose too (11.7)**, so it is the mesh's own axis and there is no lean left
to remove. The head and chest numbers are taken off exported vertices rather than
bones, because two previous runs on this rig read a picture wrongly and only settled
it by measuring a mesh.

**A fourth measurement fell out of the wings and is the sharpest of them.** The
second run recorded Nelchael's torso as *"rolled 39 degrees so that both its
shoulders sit on the same side of the centre line"*. That is visible directly in the
drawn wing vertices: before, the left wing spans X **-0.69..-0.12** and the right
**-1.39..-0.82** — both wholly on one side. After, they straddle it, **+0.19..+0.76**
and **-0.66..-0.09**. Mirror residual **1.4000 -> 0.0781** mean, **1.5686 -> 0.1001**
max, an eighteenfold improvement on a fold that was already exact to 7e-16 at the
bone level. The body was the whole of that residual.

Frame selection was checked at every level before any rule moved. Of the 47 upright

## The constant, and the trap next to it

**The bound sits in a plateau 34 degrees wide.** Anything from **6 to 39** picks the
same frame on the one rig that reaches this rung and changes nothing anywhere else;
below 6 nothing passes and it falls through, at 40 Nelchael's own 39.4 passes and the
rung does nothing. **30** is chosen inside it because it is also above every torso
lean this ladder already accepts on a two-legged rig — worst **26.8 degrees**,
skeletons 98 and 131 — and below Nelchael's 39.4, and because it is the bound the
legs are already held to. Stated as its own constant rather than a reference to
`STAND_MAX_LEG_TILT`, on the second run's own reasoning about `STAND_MAX_ARM_RISE`:
they answer different questions and only coincide today.

**A bare `BACK` substring would have been the obvious spelling and is a trap.** 68
skeletons in this cache carry `BACKSHEATHSPACER`, a sword mount pointing back and
down at 73 degrees, which would fail this test on every frame of every clip. The two
full names also pick up the numbered spines (`UPPERBACK2`, `LOWERBACK1`) and nothing
else.

## Recommendations

- **The bottom rung of a ladder is where defects go to hide.** Every rung above it
  states a condition and can be argued with; the bare gate accepted anything that
  stood, and the one rig that reached it sat there through two runs being described
  as a data limit. Worth checking the other ladders in this file for the same shape.
- The correction at the top of this entry is the fourth time here that a recorded
  *"the source cannot supply this"* turned out to be a question nobody had asked.
  Before accepting one, count the frames that pass each condition separately — 47,
  47, 34, 0 told the whole story in one line, and the 0 was the gate rather than the
  data.
- `--rest-pose` and the manifest are cheap invariants and neither one would have
  caught this class of change. What did the work was **hash-comparing every exported
  file against a worktree of HEAD**: it names the affected assets rather than
  reporting a count, and it cost under two minutes.
